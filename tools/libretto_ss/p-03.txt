4. Paragraphs

The paragraphs in the rear of this booklet form the heart of the game,
and they should not be read until called for by a Paragraph Check
or other game instruction. The paragraphs are numbered from 100
to 1000, with intentional gaps.

5. Radios

A radio may be given to your squad at the start of a Mission. If so,
it takes one Port Box to carry it. A radio is considered manned as
long as it is in a hex with an active US soldier. When a radio is
manned, a paragraph may allow you to send or receive instructions
by radio. A radio is unmanned if not in a hex with an active US
soldier, or if it has been destroyed. When a soldier who is carrying
a radio is wounded, incapacitated, or killed, by whatever means, there
is a chance that the radio is also destroyed. Roll percentile dice: On
a result of 25 or less, the radio is destroyed and is useless for the
remainder of the Mission; on any other result, there is no effect.

6. New Maps and Terrain

Silver Star includes one new full-map (Map F) and two half-maps
(Maps G and H). The Mission Set-Up instructions will tell you which
map(s) are used in each Mission and any other special instructions
that you may need.

6.1 New Terrain

Level 4. The Level 4 hexes introduced in Silver Star in no way affect
or alter the Ambush! LOS rules, except that the view from a Level
4 hill will permit you to see quite a lot of terrain.

All-Water Hexes. These hexes cannot be entered (unless specifically
allowed in a Mission briefing), and they never block LOS.
Cliffs. Only crouching soldiers can enter cliff hexes. When in a cliff
hex, a soldier cannot stand or lie prone, and he is considered to be
crouching in cover. Cliff hexes never block LOS and can be Level
2 or Level 3, as indicated on the Map Terrain Key (the only difference
between the two Level cliff hexes is in their height).

Depending on the Mission briefing, cliff hexes will be
impassable, steep, or sloping. A sloping cliff hex costs 2 MP to enter;
a steep cliff hex requires 1 Action for a soldier to climb up or down,
followed by an immediate Footing Check:

Each time a soldier (German or US) ascends or descends to a
new steep cliff hex, there is a possibility that he will lose his footing
and fall. Roll one die: On a result of 0-8, the soldier is safe; on a
result of 9, there is a possibility that the soldier failed to catch him-
self in time. Conduct an immediate PC Check; if the soldier passes
the Check, he is safe; if the soldier fails the Check, he falls to an
adjacent lower hex (determined at random), panics, and checks for
damage on the Pistol row. If the hex he falls into is another cliff hex,
conduct another PC Check to see whether or not he stops his fall
(if not, he falls into another lower hex chosen at random and suffers
damage on the Pistol row again).

Note that a panicked soldier in a cliff hex cannot fall prone and
must remain crouching. This is an exception to the standard panic
tules.

Ifa soldier is wounded, on a Footing Check die roll of 0 through
The is safe, and on a roll of 8 or 9 he loses his footing. If a soldier
is killed or incapacitated in a cliff hex, that soldier’s body falls from
cliff hex to cliff hex until it lands on a Level 1 hex closest to the
hex from which the fall began. In each cliff hex fallen through, roll
for damage on the Semi-Automatic Rifle row of the CRT for an in-
capacitated soldier; on an additional incapacitation result, the soldier
is instantly killed.

Boulders. A boulder hex is filled with large rocks. It costs a soldier
1 MP to enter a boulder hex, and a vehicle cannot enter a boulder

hex at all. A soldier in a boulder hex is considered to be in a crater
hex for purposes of determining fire combat modifiers. You might
want to pencil in Boulder next to Crater on your Fire Combat
Modifiers Chart and the MP Cost Chart. Boulders do block LOS.

Second Story Over Road. On Map F, the second story of the wooden
building in U-13 extends over the roadway. Soldiers on the road are
at Level | and in a road hex. A soldier in the second level of U-13
can see into the hex below only if he is standing, and soldiers in the
road can see only standing targets in the building above. LOS from
the second level of U-13 to adjacent and other hexes follows normal
Ambush! rules for LOS.

7. New Counters

In addition to providing more counters of types that you are familiar
with (from playing Ambush!), Silver Star offers new counter types
that are necessary for play.

Destroyed Wall Hexside. When a wall hexside is destroyed by a
bazooka or tank main gun, place one of these markers in a hex with
the arrow on the marker facing the destroyed hexside. This will help
you to remember which hexside is now open. Remember that, if a
second hexside of the same building hex is destroyed, the building
hex turns into rubble (see 13.5 in Ambush!).

JAM

Jam. These markers are used as generic jam markers when certain
weapon type counters are unavailable.

Vehicle AR Markers. Some new vehicles are provided with double-
letter codes on their counters, and they have matching double-letter
coded AR markers with IN ratings from 5 to 0 to reflect crew losses.
When a vehicle suffers a crew loss, invert or replace the AR marker
so that the new lower IN is exposed, and place the marker in the
new correct IN column of the AR Track. The German vehicles receive
Turns in alphabetical order, with double-letter AR markers follow-
ing single-letter markers. (For example, GE L would go, then GE
LL, followed by GE M.) You decide the order in which US vehicles
and soldiers receive Turns.
