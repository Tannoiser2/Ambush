1. Introduction

Silver Star is an even tougher test for your battle-weary campaign
veterans than the original Ambush! and its later modules, Purple Heart
and Move Out! Each Mission is specifically designed to attempt to
eliminate a certain number of your squad members; in fact, you should
be advised that it is possible to lose your entire squad in a matter
of minutes should your playmanship falter, or should your nerve
crack! Players are encouraged to brush up on a few of your favorite
Missions from Ambush! or any of the modules before taking on the
challenge of Silver Star.

. The best advice we can give is to suggest that you read the Hints
and Reminders section very carefully, as these clues represent the
best we and our playtesters can offer you on how to get back home
alive.

In Silver Star, new rules, clever German tricks, and special effects
have been buried in the paragraphs in an attempt to maximize sur-
prise; in fact, players begin most Missions with no hint of purpose
other than to enter the map — making uncertainty the rule of the game
and key to the fun. Players are once again encouraged not to break
the system by looking over the paragraphs and other supports for
hints or clues as to what lies ahead. Remember that part of the fun
is not knowing — until it is almost too late!

Your squad is invited to campaign its way through Silver Star,
and should anyone from the original squad survive, they will be
assured of their well-deserved rewards as defenders of America,
freedom, and democracy. We hope you enjoy Silver Star and that
it provides the additional challenges your squad deserves.

A WORD ON REPLAYABILITY

The Missions in Silver Star are almost infinitely replayable. It is quite
possible that your intrepid soldiers will not win a Mission the first
time around, but don’t despair. Give it another shot, because the folks
at home are counting on you. Even if you do win, each Mission con-
tains many surprises, and it is unlikely that you will uncover them
all the first time around. Also, in many cases, German movement
and Activation is somewhat randomly controlled, so they will not
do the same things every time you play. (This means that even if
you remember where the Germans appeared last time, and set up
an ambush for them, they can still surprise you. Beware!)

It is suggested that you try something different each time you
replay a Mission. You can radically alter the way a Mission goes
simply by changing where you enter and move, or even by changing
the way you outfit your squad.

2. US Vehicles

2.1 Independent US Vehicles

Many of the US vehicles you encounter in Silver Star are not under
your command. Instead, they move and fight just as German vehicles
do, following all movement and combat rules just as if they were
German (with the exception that the US vehicles will not attack your

squad). US Vehicle Cards contain Condition, Action Die Roll, and
Paragraph references which direct the vehicles’ Actions. Movement
is conducted from hex to hex, using the current Condition Card move-
ment references, moving the vehicle to the hex indicated as if the
vehicle were German. However, these vehicles do make Paragraph
Checks, just as US soldiers. In all cases, the current German Action
Number is used to determine the US vehicle Actions. ‘

Remain in Rounds as long as active US vehicles not under your
control are on the map, even if there are no active Germans on the
map. New paragraphs have been included to assure that the US
vehicles will not kill your squad members (but careless soldiers can
be run over). Should you wish to use these vehicles for your own
purposes, resist the temptation. Do not, for instance, put incapacitated
soldiers in a US jeep that is not under your control. If your squad
begins a Mission with control over a vehicle, you can use that vehicle
as described in Section 17 of the Ambush! rules.

2.2 New Tank Actions

There are two new tank Actions (see 17.13 of the Ambush! rules)
available to the tanks in Silver Star: pushing and crunching. These
Actions are not specifically called for by paragraph. The tanks will
automatically perform one or the other when circumstances dictate
(just as a tank is not instructed by paragraph to try to run over a slow
soldier in its path, but it does so if given the opportunity).

Pushing Vehicles. If a US or German tank begins its Turn adjacent
to a hex containing 2 immobile large vehicles and is instructed to
enter their hex, it will attempt to push one out of its way. (If a tank
encounters the two vehicles during movement, it will stop moving
in an adjacent hex and, the next time it is instructed to move, it will
push.) The pushing tank makes an Accident Check. If the tank fails
the Check, its push attempt fails and the tank remains in the hex it
attempted to push from; roll on the Accident Table and apply any
results immediately. If the tank passes the Check, the vehicle being
pushed is moved to the adjacent hex that would cost a tank the least
Movement Points to enter (clear before cover; cover before rough;
and so forth) and the pushing tank is moved into the hex vacated by
the vehicle that is pushed aside. A vehicle cannot be pushed into a
hex that a tank is prohibited from entering, nor can it be pushed into
the hex from which the tank is pushing. A tank that is attempting
to push a vehicle aside can perform no other Action in that Turn.

Crunching Vehicles. A tank that is entering a hex that contains a
small vehicle occupied by active enemy targets will attempt to run
over the vehicle, making 2 Accident Checks: one just prior to the
crunch, and the other immediately following. If the tank passes the
first Check, the small vehicle is immediately disabled. Each passenger
then conducts an immediate PC Check. A passenger who passes his
PC Check is placed prone and unharmed in the same hex; a passenger
who fails his PC Check rolls for damage on the Satchel Charge Out-
side row. If the tank fails the first Check, it fails to crunch the vehicle
and loses the rest of its Turn (although it does not suffer an accident).
If the tank has successfully crunched a vehicle, then it makes a second
Accident Check; if it fails this Check, it has an Accident.

If a tank is instructed to enter a hex containing two vehicles,
at least one of which is small and is not occupied by friendly active
soldiers, the tank can enter that hex by crunching one vehicle.

3. Game Parts

Silver Star provides new counters, three new maps (one full-map and
two half-maps), new Mission Cards, new German and US cards, and
this Rules/Paragraph booklet. It is necessary to own the original
Ambush! counters, view sleeve, and dice to play Silver Star. You
will not need the original Ambush! Paragraph Booklet, German Cards,
or Mission Cards, nor is it necessary to own a copy of any of the
other modules (although, if you enjoy playing Ambush! we suggest
that you run right out and buy Move Out! and Purple Heart).
