closer than 7 hexes (exclusive) from its
location. The mortar fire can scatter into a
hex less than 7 hexes away during hit
resolution. When a mortar team member is
directed to fire the mortar, first check for dud
using a dud number of 05. If the round is not
a dud, use the first of the following to apply:

527. The mules are loaded with ammo and
supplies for the front lines (these supplies
cannot be used by your squad).

Control. Mule counters have two sides,
marked under control and unattended, to help
you keep track of their status. A mule is
under control when an active soldier in the
same hex picks up the lead rope (no cost in
Operations; 1 MP in Rounds); flip the mule
counter to its under control side when this
happens. One soldier can hold up to 4 mules
in place (keep them under control without
moving them). One soldier can move only 1
or 2 mules at a time. Once a mule is under
control, it stays under control until the soldier
controling the mule drops the lead rope,
which he can do at any time at no cost; if this
occurs, flip the mule counter to the unattended
side. If the soldier controlling the mule is
wounded, incapacitated, or killed, he drops
the lead rope automatically; flip the mule
counter to its unattended side. A soldier
controlling a mule must drop the lead rope
(flip counter) to fire a bazooka, LMG, or
BAR, or to make or defend against an assault.
A soldier controlling a mule can only snap
fire weapons other than those named above
unless he drops the lead rope (flip counter)
before firing.

Movement. Mules will not move unless being
led by an active soldier or unless directed by
paragraph. One soldier can lead up to 2 mules
under his control. The soldier moves normally
and the mules move with him. Mules cannot
enter cliff hexes and can enter and exit
buildings only through doors or destroyed
wall hexsides. No more than 4 mules can be
in any hex at one time after they have been
unloaded from the barge.

Damage to Mules. When mules are hit by
fire or subject to grenade attack, roll 1 die:
On a result of 0-3, there is no effect; on a

result of 4-7, the mule panics and will not
move for the rest of the Round; on a result of
8, the mule is wounded (place marker) and
will not enter more than 2 MP worth of hexes
per Turn, regardless of how many MPs the
soldier controlling the mule has to spend; on a
result of 9, the mule is incapacitated (place
marker). If a mule is incapacitated as a résult
of rolling a 9 (but not as the result of being
wounded twice), roll 1 die: On a result of
0-8, there is no effect; on a result of 9, the
supplies on the mule explode, the mule is
killed (remove it from the map), and all
soldiers in the hex suffer damage on the
Satchel Charge Outside row. Any other mules
in the hex suffer damage as in the beginning
of this section (more than one mule may have
its supplies explode as the result of a single
attack).

Combat Against Soldiers in a Hex With
Mules. Unless otherwise directed, when a
German fires at a hex with both soldiers and
mules in it, the target is a soldier. A mule is
considered a soldier for the purposes of
resolving Aimed Automatic Weapon Fire (see
Ambush!), but soldiers in the hex suffer
damage first. If a German is directed
specifically to fire at a mule, the mule is
considered a standing soldier in the terrain it
‘occupies. A prone soldier in a hex with an
incapacitated mule is —2 to hit, unless the
terrain offers a better modifier. If a prone
soldier in a hex with an incapacitated mule is
fired on and missed, roll 1 die: On a result of
0-8, no effect; on a result of 9, the supplies
on the incapacitated mule explode and damage
is resolved as above. If a hex with soldiers
and mules in it is subjected to a grenade
attack, all soldiers and mules in the hex could
incur damage. Soldiers resolve the grenade
attack normally, and the mules take damage as
above. Mule counters are marked with 527 on
both sides to remind you of this paragraph for
easy reference.
